    Run the workspace full screen

    This tool is laid out like a CAD workspace: a 3D viewport, object browser, properties and checks all on one screen. In a window the panels get cramped and the viewport is small.

    Switch any time from View › Full screen, the button in the top bar, or F11.

    About this model

    Link-segment manikin from individual anthropometric records. Posture is solved from the workstation geometry; all dimensions in mm.

    Mouse and keys

    • Left-drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-drag (or Shift-drag) to pan. Drag the view cube to turn, or click a face, edge or corner to snap to it.
    • The browser at the top left of the viewport lists every object. Use the eye to show or hide it; hover a row to outline the object.
    • F11 (Windows) or Ctrl+Cmd+F (Mac) toggles full screen; Esc leaves it.

    Model assumptions

    • The body is a link-segment model built from the selected record's measurements. It has no soft-tissue compression, the seat is treated as a hard flat surface (as in the survey seated measurements), and posture is set by the workstation geometry, not by a validated posture-prediction model.
    • Hip joint: half buttock depth forward of the back of the buttock, half thigh clearance above the seat. Knee joint: midway between buttock–popliteal and buttock–knee lengths, and halfway between popliteal height and knee height. Ankle joint: lateral malleolus height.
    • Shoulder joint: 11% of shoulder–elbow length below the acromion. Upper-arm link: 82% of shoulder–elbow length. Forearm link: radiale–stylion (or elbow–wrist) length. On a keyboard the fingertips reach the home row at 75% of hand length from the wrist, wrist 30 mm above the key tops.
    • Reach zones are drawn on the desk at 30 mm above its surface. Normal zone: forearm plus 40% of hand length (to the centre of grip), swung from a relaxed elbow position below each shoulder. Maximum zone: upper arm plus forearm plus 40% of hand length, swung from each shoulder. Both assume no trunk lean.
    • Screens: side-by-side screens are hinged edge to edge at the set angle and gap, symmetrically about the chosen centre. Curved screens bend about a vertical axis at their stated radius. The head pitches to view the main screen; it does not turn, so horizontal angles are reported as gaze angles from straight ahead.
    • Seated shoulder and cervicale heights for ANSUR II are derived per subject as standing height minus (stature − sitting height), because ANSUR II has no seated acromion height.
    • Neck flexion pivots at C7. In auto head mode the head tilts until the eyes look down at the main screen centre by the preferred eye rotation, limited to −10° to 45°. Head inclination 0° means the Frankfurt plane is level.
    • Thighs rest on the seat unless the knees are forced higher by the feet. If the seat is higher than popliteal height plus shoe, the feet hang. Implied wrist extension assumes the hand lies flat on the keyboard or device.
    • ANSUR II is a 2012 survey of US Army personnel. It is the strongest public individual-level dataset but reflects a fit military population, and male and female groups should be analysed separately.

    Regression mode uses ordinary least squares on stature; low R² values and derived dimensions are flagged in the dimension table.

    Export drawing

    A3 landscape sheet. Right side elevation, front elevation and plan are aligned for the chosen projection at one standard scale. Dimensions in mm. The setup summary, parts list and flagged results sit above the title block. The DXF has the same sheet as vector linework with hidden lines removed, on named layers, drawn at full size so the views measure true millimetres.

    DXF files can't be saved from inside Claude. Open the tool on its own site to export DXF.
